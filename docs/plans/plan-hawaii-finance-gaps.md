# Hawaii finance gaps

## Source and callpath findings

The [Commission's candidate data guide](https://ags.hawaii.gov/campaign/cc/view-searchable-data/) links published Schedule A contributions and the actual candidate filing system. The [noncandidate guide](https://ags.hawaii.gov/campaign/nc/view-searchable-data/) separates contributions to spenders, contributions made to candidates, and other expenditures. Those are different financial edges. The existing client uses candidate dataset `jexd-xbcg` and noncandidate expense dataset `riiu-7d4b`; `rajm-32md` contains the spenders' own funders.

IE is already called by the sync, but occupations, receipt sizes and IE share a `Promise.all` after direct candidate resolution and total hydration. A direct failure can suppress outside processing. The IE query also filters positive amounts and known stances before qualification, skips ambiguous/invalid rows, and caps groups at 20 before summary totals. Independent acquisition and full validation must precede presentation limits. The batch's legacy summary clock and one-day post-election grace do not establish independent IE freshness.

Candidate receipts expose explicit `contributor_type` values: Noncandidate Committee, Political Party, Candidate, Individual, Immediate Family and Other Entity. Current direct calls return occupation and size aggregates rather than named committees. Generic organizations and recipient filer type are not donor committee authority. The full committee rule requires resolving party and candidate donor meanings rather than silently choosing only the easiest class. Candidate own funds belong on Schedule C or D, according to the [candidate guidebook](https://ags.hawaii.gov/campaign/files/2026/04/CC-Guidebook.pdf); a Schedule A label of Candidate must not automatically be treated as self-funding. Contribution refunds appear on expenditure Schedule B, so positive Schedule A cash/in-kind contributions are not net committee funding.
